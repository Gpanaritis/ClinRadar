# Tie every supply risk to a shipment

*Research report, 15 November 2026. Written from web research (sources linked inline) and a read-only review of this repository at commit 6d04e99, when the product was still RegChain AI. Repository links point to that commit of the private repository and need GitHub access.*

> **Since this report.** The SupplyShield change ([SupplyShield-AI.md](SupplyShield-AI.md)) acts on several of its findings. External events are now matched to shipments, facilities, routes and deadlines, in three exposure states with their evidence. The monitoring scope comes from the active-trial portfolio, and extra countries are recommended with reasons, never switched on. Invented events are labelled "illustrative scenario" on screen. UK FCDO advice and GDACS alerts are read live. Import approval terms (port of entry, kits, consignee, validity) are checked per shipment. The demo script no longer claims a live run that has not happened. Two findings stand as written: no live Claude run is on record yet, and pending regulatory changes still hold shipments. SupplyShield holds shipments against a pending change, the safe side, while the Country Specification itself changes only after the Regulatory SME approves. The report's alternative, showing pending changes as a what-if, is a decision for the team.


The clinical supply problem behind RegChain AI is real, but RegChain aimed at its least-evidenced slice, missed regulatory changes, and built that slice mostly as a simulation, so the move to SupplyShield AI is the right one if SupplyShield sells shipment-level exposure under human control and proves it on customer data. Public data measures only one supply failure well: waste, with **about 45% of manufactured investigational product reaching patients** against planned overage of **150–200%**. The cross-border leg is the weak link (**80% on-time delivery into in-country depots versus 97% to sites**), yet no source gives a per-shipment rate for customs holds, permit failures or temperature excursions, and delay is costed only per trial-day (**$55,716 in Phase III**), never per held shipment. No regulation mandates a continuity tool, but ICH E6(R3) makes uninterrupted treatment a sponsor duty, the FDA asks sponsors whether their contingency plans fit foreseeable disruptions, and the draft EU GMP Annex 22 would keep LLMs out of critical GMP decisions. The best-measured shocks are wars that shut airspace and hubs, touching **4.4% of pivotal-trial sites in 2022 and 6.7% of recruiting trials in 2026**, often through hubs in countries with no trial sites, and no vendor ties such an event, or a country rule, to a specific IMP shipment, facility, route or deadline; Veeva is best placed to close that gap. RegChain has one real UK rule, a fictional portfolio, **315 recorded agent runs, all scripted**, monitoring screens that replay samples without demo labels, and a leak that lets unapproved country changes drive shipment checks. SupplyShield should keep RegChain's approval and audit machinery, close that leak, label every simulation, run its agents live and prove its value by replaying the dated disruptions of 2022–26 against a pilot customer's real shipments; the app's user-experience review is covered separately.

## Waste is the only measured failure; border failures go uncounted

Investigational product (IMP) moves from the manufacturer through packaging and labelling, QP certification and central or regional depots to in-country depots, then to sites or patients. The only multi-company benchmark of that chain, a Tufts CSDD study of 12 companies, 73 studies and 5,682 sites using 2014–15 data, found **42% of studies distributing centrally, 41% through regional hubs and 16% through local depots**; 43 of the 73 outsourced logistics, about 70% used an IRT, and couriers plus depots took **49% of logistics cost** ([ACT/Tufts](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/assessing-global-clinical-supply-logistics)). The knowledge needed to ship lawfully is scattered. In South Africa the trial approval letter serves as the import authority, Nigeria's permit fixes quantities and limits supply to a named investigator and site, and Kenya issues a separate permit ([Pharmaceutical Online](https://www.pharmaceuticalonline.com/doc/the-reliance-trap-when-regulatory-strategy-becomes-a-clinical-supply-problem-0001)); Oman makes the site itself the importer of record ([DIA Global Forum](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/march-2026/navigating-global-clinical-supply-challenges-in-diverse-regulatory-environments/)). Expiry windows and depot-to-country routing live in the IRT, permit quantities with local importers, approved labels with sponsor regulatory teams and batch certification with the QP. No public source describes one system holding all of it, so any shipment-level check, SupplyShield's included, is first a problem of joining data held by three or four parties.

Waste is the one failure with measured, non-vendor numbers. In the Tufts data 90% of manufactured product was packaged, 74% of that was shipped and 67.8% of shipped product was dispensed, so **roughly 45% of what is made reaches a patient** ([ACT/Tufts](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/assessing-global-clinical-supply-logistics)). Consultants put administration near 40% against planned overage of **150–200%** ([ZS](https://www.zs.com/content/dam/pdfs/whitepaper-rethinking-waste-management-in-clinical-supply-chains.pdf)), median kit waste sits near 50% ([ContractPharma](https://www.contractpharma.com/clinical-supply-chain-resilience-in-a-new-operating-reality/)), and one vendor case cut a trial's cost from **$85M to $76M** by trimming comparator overage from 65% to 52% ([N-SIDE](https://www.n-side.com/en/insights/optimize-comparator-sourcing-in-clinical-trials-by-reducing-overage/)). Overage is the industry's continuity insurance, bought in bulk because the risks it covers are not measured shipment by shipment.

The cross-border leg looks like the weak point. Tufts recorded **97% on-time delivery to sites but only 80% into in-country depots**, without attributing causes, and named Argentina, Russia, China, Colombia and India as the worst for licensing delays ([ACT/Tufts](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/assessing-global-clinical-supply-logistics)). Practitioner accounts from 2025–26 describe the mechanisms. Argentina requires a revised licence for any change in shipment contents, which can add weeks, and Saudi Arabia's exact-kit invoices and pre-shipment licence cut lab-kit shelf life to four months ([DIA Global Forum](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/march-2026/navigating-global-clinical-supply-challenges-in-diverse-regulatory-environments/)). South African border control cannot release IMP when the approval letter omits quantities ([Pharmaceutical Online](https://www.pharmaceuticalonline.com/doc/the-reliance-trap-when-regulatory-strategy-becomes-a-clinical-supply-problem-0001)), import licences often cannot be filed until the country approves the trial, adding weeks to start-up ([TIRS 2020](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s43441-020-00207-2)), and Latin American holds trace to missing pre-approvals and inconsistent paperwork ([PharmTech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/q-a-how-can-sponsors-respond-to-clinical-trial-customs-delays-)). The widely repeated claim that **about 60% of customs delays come from documentation errors has no traceable dataset**, and the two outlets that carry it disagree on whether it counts delays or holds ([SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/blogs/1-think-tank/post/42700-the-high-stakes-of-customs-compliance-in-healthcare-shipping); [World Pharma Today](https://www.worldpharmatoday.com/?p=34530)). Temperature excursions were the leading cause of product-impact events in the Tufts data and took **57 hours on average to resolve** (range 6–114) ([ACT/Tufts](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/assessing-global-clinical-supply-logistics)), while the popular "$35B a year" cold-chain loss figure traces only to a 2019 packaging-vendor survey ([Air Cargo News](https://www.aircargonews.net/?p=1023281)). Labelling errors are rare but severe: Nigerian customs rejected German-labelled saline ([DIA Global Forum](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/march-2026/navigating-global-clinical-supply-challenges-in-diverse-regulatory-environments/)), and an MHRA inspection found blister labels so incomplete that the dose could not be traced, and one site halted recruitment ([MHRA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64357bfe89f19f00133cfb40/GCP_inspection_metrics_2019-2020.pdf)). Inspectors keep finding IMP accountability failures, the **fourth most common FDA observation for investigators and third for sponsors and CROs in FY2023** ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/07/alerts-lifesciences-common-fda-bioresearch-monitoring-violations)), but no inspection programme measures customs or permit problems.

| Failure mode | Frequency evidence | Cost evidence | Who usually catches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overage and waste | ≈45% of product reaches patients; 150–200% overage planned | $85M→$76M in one vendor case | Supply planners, forecasting tools, IRT |
| IMP accountability and storage | FDA FY2023 rank 4 (investigators) and rank 3 (sponsors/CROs); MHRA critical findings | Not quantified | Site pharmacy, monitors, inspectors |
| Temperature excursions | Leading cause of product-impact events; no per-shipment rate | 57 h mean to resolve | Courier loggers, depot QA, IRT quarantine |
| Import permits | Consistent but qualitative reports | Weeks per licence revision; lost shelf life | Local importer or broker, often at the border |
| Customs holds | 80% vs 97% on-time; "60%" untraceable | No primary figure | Broker or courier |
| Labelling errors | Among the least common causes | Recruitment halted at one site | Depot QA, QP, investigator |
| Site stock-outs | No data | One vendor case | IRT resupply, site |

For RegChain's original thesis the decisive gap is that **no source separates holds caused by changed rules from holds caused by existing rules that were missed**. The recent cases (Argentina's licence revisions, Oman's importer-of-record rule, Nigeria's label language, Saudi invoices) involve standing requirements, and South Africa's quantity reminder enforces an old one ([DIA Global Forum](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/march-2026/navigating-global-clinical-supply-challenges-in-diverse-regulatory-environments/); [Pharmaceutical Online](https://www.pharmaceuticalonline.com/doc/the-reliance-trap-when-regulatory-strategy-becomes-a-clinical-supply-problem-0001)). Rules do change. One search pass found **at least nine supply-relevant changes in eight jurisdictions between 2022 and 2026**, a floor rather than a rate, and several remove burdens: the EU made the expiry date optional on immediate packaging in specified cases from December 2022 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2022/2239/oj/eng)), Brazil now issues the import document within 30 business days of filing ([Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/en/unico/updates-clinical-trial-regulations/)), and China introduced a single customs clearance for multiple uses ([Cisema](https://cisema.com/news/nmpa-urgent-drugs-2026)). Major changes come with long notice and often turn on facts that can be checked per shipment. The UK's new labelling regime applies to IMP QP-certified from **28 April 2026**, ten months after guidance first appeared ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/clinical-trials-for-medicines-labelling); [GOV.UK transitional](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/clinical-trials-regulations-transitional-arrangements)), and the 2026 US Section 232 tariff zero-rates goods solely for clinical trials only with intended-use documentation before entry ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20261015-section-232-pharmaceutical-tariffs-are-now-in-effect-its-not-too-late-to-consider-duty-mitigation-strategies)). The free NIH ClinRegs service covers 23 countries and reviews profiles roughly once a year ([ClinRegs](https://clinregs.niaid.nih.gov/about)); it logged seven updates between late July and early November 2026, none about IMP import or labelling ([ClinRegs updates](https://clinregs.niaid.nih.gov/updates/list)). Rule changes are episodic, often relaxations, and outnumbered in the case record by misapplied rules and quiet enforcement shifts, so a rule-change feed on its own covers the smaller share of the problem.

Cost evidence is thin where it matters most. Tufts CSDD puts the direct cost of a trial day at **$55,716 in Phase III, $23,737 in Phase II, $14,091 in Phase IV and $7,829 in Phase I** (2023 dollars, excluding sponsor overhead) ([ACT, Getz 2024](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/how-much-does-a-day-of-delay-in-a-clinical-trial-really-cost-)). Lost sales come out at **about $500,000 a day in one Tufts output and $800,000 in another**, both citing the same 2024 paper, with therapy-area medians from $840,000 for oncology to $1.4M for cardiovascular drugs ([ACT](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/how-much-does-a-day-of-delay-in-a-clinical-trial-really-cost-); [Tufts white paper](https://csdd.tufts.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Aug2024%20Day%20of%20Delay%20White%20Paper%20Final.pdf)). These are averages over a drug's market life, a single-country hold rarely sits on a trial's critical path, and **no primary figure exists for the cost of a customs hold, a relabelling campaign or a stock-out**. Any credible business case has to come from a customer's own hold and disruption history.

## Continuity rules judge outcomes, and the worst shocks arrive through hubs

### E6(R3) demands unbroken treatment without ever saying "business continuity"

No GCP or GMP text applies "business continuity" to IMP supply, and the phrase appears nowhere in ICH E6(R3). The obligation is framed by outcome instead. Section 3.15.3(c)(i) requires timely supply of IMP so the trial is not interrupted and to secure the **"continuation of treatment for participants"**; 3.10.1.1 requires risks to be identified before and throughout the trial, naming investigational product administration and service-provider activities, with periodic review under 3.10.1.5; 3.13.3 demands prompt action on immediate hazards; and 4.3.3(d) and 4.3.6 require tested disaster recovery and contingency for data essential to participant safety ([ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6%28R3%29_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf)), which the EU applied from July 2025 ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/ich-e6-good-clinical-practice-scientific-guideline)). EU GMP Annex 11 §16 asks for documented, tested fallback arrangements when a computerised system breaks down ([Annex 11](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)), and the MHRA graded as critical a case where an IRT audit trail was lost because the vendor had decommissioned the system ([MHRA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64357bfe89f19f00133cfb40/GCP_inspection_metrics_2019-2020.pdf)). ICH Q9(R1) added a section on product-availability risk, with sole suppliers as an example vulnerability ([RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/ich-releases-revised-q9-guideline-to-improve-risk.html); [ISPE](https://ispe.org/pharmaceutical-engineering/september-october-2023/risk-management-avoidance-drug-shortages)). The FDA's September 2023 guidance on trials during disasters comes closest to a continuity checklist: its first answer tells sponsors to assess the continued availability of supplies and vendors, shelf life included, and to ask whether current contingency plans fit the disruptions they can anticipate; later answers allow home delivery, shipment to local providers and local commercial sourcing, and let immediate-hazard changes precede IRB approval ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-conduct-clinical-trials-medical-products-during-major-disruptions-due-disasters-and)). The EU's COVID-19 guidance progressively allowed direct-to-patient shipping and larger home supplies ([ECA](https://gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/updated-guidance-on-the-management-of-clinical-trials-during-cov19)), and the CTCG's April 2022 recommendation on Ukraine made participants' continuous access to treatment the primary objective, served from local EU/EEA supply ([CTCG](https://www.anm.ro/en/_/ANUNTURI%20IMPORTANTE/2022_04_CTCG_recommendation_to_sponsors_on_managing_the_impact_of_the_war_in_Ukraine_on_clinical_trials.pdf)).

The EMA's serious-breach guideline shows why SupplyShield's split between observed events, potential exposures and confirmed disruptions matters legally. Its examples include participants systematically missing IMP doses, dosing with IMP whose stability after an unmanaged excursion cannot be verified, and IRT faults that lead to expired IMP being dispensed and sites running short; an excursion is not a breach if the product was quarantined and qualified staff documented the assessment ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-notification-serious-breaches-regulation-eu-no-5362014-or-clinical-trial-protocol_en.pdf)). Serious breaches must be reported within seven days under Article 52 of the Clinical Trials Regulation ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/536/oj)). An observed event carries no obligation, a potential exposure belongs in the periodic risk review and the FDA's contingency question, and a confirmed disruption can open a deviation or start a seven-day clock. **A tool that blurs the three states either floods teams with escalations or buries reportable facts.**

The same texts favour advice over automation. The draft EU GMP Annex 22 covers only static, deterministic AI models in critical GMP uses, excludes generative AI and LLMs from its scope with an instruction to keep them out of critical GMP applications, and leaves qualified staff responsible for AI outputs in non-critical uses ([PIC/S draft](https://picscheme.org/docview/9715)). The draft had not been adopted by November 2026 ([Pharmaceutical Online](https://www.pharmaceuticalonline.com/doc/what-europe-s-draft-annex-actually-asks-of-human-oversight-of-ai-0001)), and a trade analysis after EMA's mid-2026 generative-AI workshop questions whether the LLM ban will survive ([PharmTech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/europe-tried-to-ban-generative-ai-from-critical-gmp-the-ban-may-not-survive-it-does-not-matter-)), but no sponsor can plan on that. The EU Critical Medicines Act (provisional deal 12 May 2026) and the pharmaceutical package (11 December 2025) target marketed products, and their summaries do not mention IMPs ([McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/pharma-and-life-sciences/eu-critical-medicines-act-political-agreement-reached-what-life-sciences-companies-need-to-know); [Pharmaceutical-Tech](https://www.pharmaceutical-tech.com/news/eu-co-legislators-reach-provisional-agreement-on-comprehensive-reform-of-pharmaceutical-legislation)), although a 2025 trade headline suggested investigational products could be swept in ([Pink Sheet](https://insights.citeline.com/pink-sheet/compliance/distribution-supply-chain/eu-critical-medicines-act-may-include-investigational-products-mandatory-redistribution-measure-VX6TUTOZSNEEZJIJTPDIO2CNAI)).

### Two wars in four years touched 4–7% of the global trial footprint

Wars that close airspace and hubs are the best-measured continuity shock. When Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, Russia hosted 1,243 active trials at 10,158 sites and Ukraine 612 trials at 5,015 sites ([Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/the-impact-of-the-russia-ukraine-war-on-clinical-trials-0001)); **4.4% of recruiting pivotal-trial sites (2,911 of 65,593)** were in the two countries ([Outsourcing-Pharma](https://www.outsourcing-pharma.com/Article/2022/03/24/ukraine-war-dramatically-impacting-global-clinical-development)), Ukrainian oncology trial registrations fell from 41 in 2021 to 3 in 2022 ([MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2227-9032/11/3/283)), and IMP was flown into neighbouring countries ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/newsletters/experts-call-for-a-shift-from-reactive-to-proactive-clinical-trial-supply-frameworks)). Israel closed its airspace from 13 to 22 June 2025 ([ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/155232)), and a clinical courier held imports until flights resumed by 11 July ([Marken](https://www.marken.com/news/supply-chain-operational-update-israel-jordan-lebanon)). In March 2026 the US/Israel–Iran war shut the Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Doha hubs; pharma cargo was rerouted through China or Singapore or flown to Saudi Arabia or Oman and trucked on, and some customers feared running out within four to six weeks ([Dawn/Reuters](https://www.dawn.com/news/1982633)). A vendor analysis in April 2026, not independently verified, counted **4,361 of 65,061 recruiting trials (6.7%) and 7,958 sites affected**, led by Turkey, Israel and Egypt ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/april-2026/analysis-shows-nearly-7-percent-of-global-clinical-trials-are-impacted-by-middle-east-conflict/)). One survey found only 22% of pharma firms preparing for such disruption at the end of 2025 ([EMJ](https://www.emjreviews.com/emj-gold/news/supply-chains-and-trials-hit-by-middle-east-instability/)).

Other shocks are documented with less trial-level precision. COVID-19 cut lung-cancer trial enrollment by 43% between 2019 and 2020 in one analysis ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20210909/lung-cancer-trial-enrollment-dropped-43-amid-covid19-pandemic)), and suspended enrollment accounted for 67.3% of disrupted trials ([GlobalData](https://www.globaldata.com/67-3-of-clinical-trials-disrupted-by-covid-19-are-due-to-enrollment-suspension/)). Comparator and background-drug shortages come next: in 2023, 93% of 27 US cancer centres lacked carboplatin and 70% cisplatin, and in 2024 shortages affected trials at 43% of 28 centres, 58% of which saw lower enrollment ([Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20240626/platinum-chemotherapy-supply-rises-but-new-concerns-about-cancer-drug-shortages-emerge); [ACT](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/national-comprehensive-cancer-network-survey-drug-shortages-clinical-trials)). Cyber incidents are real but rarely measured in trials. NotPetya cost Merck $285M in manufacturing costs and forced it to borrow Gardasil 9 doses from the CDC stockpile ([SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000310158/000031015818000005/mrk1231201710k.htm)), and ransomware at a trial-software vendor slowed trials for about two weeks while sites tracked patients on paper ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2020/10/04/covid-19-clinical-trials-hit-ransomware-targets-medical-software-company/)). Hurricane Helene knocked out about 60% of US IV-fluid supply ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/hospitals-take-steps-conserve-iv-fluid-supply-helene-strikes-critical-rcna173861)) and the April 2025 Iberian blackout kept Catalan hospitals on generators for 24–50 hours ([ARA](https://en.ara.cat/society/nearly-5-000-medical-tests-and-500-operations-postponed-due-to-the-general-blackout_1_5363248.html)), but neither produced trial data. Policy shocks change paperwork more than cost: the 2026 Section 232 tariff of up to 100% on patented drugs zero-rates trial goods identified by IND number ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/trump-administration-imposes-section-232-tariffs-on-patented-pharmaceutical-imports-tiered-rate-structure-takes-effect-beginning-july-31-2026)), and BIOSECURE, signed on 18 December 2025, awaits a list of restricted companies due by 18 December 2026 ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/united-states-the-biosecure-act-becomes-law)). No trial-level data was found for the Red Sea crisis or the October 2024 US port strike.

Three lessons carry into product design. First, shocks strike nodes such as airspace, hubs and single plants, and the exposed shipments often belong to trials with no site in the affected country, as the Gulf hub closures showed, so **a monitor scoped only to trial countries misses them**. Second, impact is reported as trials or sites "affected", which is exposure; missed doses are almost never counted, so the industry's own figures merge the states SupplyShield wants to separate. Third, the recoveries that worked (rerouting, serving relocated participants from local supply, direct-to-patient delivery and higher site buffers where disruption looms) each fall under a different rule set, so mitigation advice has to be rule-aware ([ContractPharma](https://www.contractpharma.com/?p=13465)).

## Every vendor sells a piece; none ties an outside event to an IMP shipment

Seven product categories touch the problem, and each stops short of the shipment.

| Category | Examples | What it ties risk to | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulatory intelligence | RegASK, Freyr Ria, IQVIA RIM Smart, Clarivate Cortellis, NIH ClinRegs | Products, markets, ingredients, submissions, documents | No shipment, lot or permit data |
| RTSM/IRT and planning | Oracle Clinical One, N-SIDE, Veeva, Suvoda, Signant, Endpoint | Lots, depots and countries, as fixed configuration | Changes only through manual change orders |
| RTSM agents | Signant Forecasting & Planning Assist, Endpoint elo, Suvoda agentic RTSM | Expiry, lead-time and stock-out risk inside one system | No external events, no regulatory scope |
| Commercial release | Veeva Batch Release | Batches to markets with open issues | Commercial products only |
| Trade compliance | SAP GTS, ONESOURCE, Descartes | Licences drawn down by quantity | Built for export control, no IMP workflow |
| Couriers and importers of record | Marken, Thermo Fisher | Import requirements and documents | Knowledge stays inside the service |
| Supply-chain risk monitoring | Everstream, Resilinc, Prewave, Exiger | Suppliers and external events | No IMP, lot or trial mapping |

Regulatory intelligence has turned into an agent race. RegASK checks impact against products, ingredients, claims and markets with parallel agents whose output experts validate ([RegASK](https://regask.com/ai-info-page/)); Freyr's Ria agent draws on more than 130,000 regulations and lists supply chain among the functions it serves ([Freyr](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/meet-ria-regulatory-impact-assessment-agent)); IQVIA describes agents that map changes to affected documents ([IQVIA](https://www.iqvia.com/blogs/2026/06/how-agentic-ai-transforms-regulatory-intelligence)); and Clarivate opened its Cortellis AI assistant to all customers in December 2025 ([Clarivate](https://clarivate.com/news/clarivate-presents-cortellis-regulatory-ai-assistant/)). None maps a change to a shipment, lot or permit, and **detection alone is becoming a commodity**.

RTSMs hold fixed per-country rules. Oracle's label groups set which countries a lot may ship to, release 21.3 added per-country do-not-ship and do-not-count windows, and a 24.2 setting lets depot-to-depot moves bypass do-not-ship limits ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/health-sciences/irt-cloud/user-guide/172838.htm); [21.3](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/life-sciences/clinical-one/change-log/21.3-customize-dns-and-dnc-values.html); [24.2](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/life-sciences/clinical-one/change-log/24-2-allow-depot-depot-shipments-staging-kits.html)). Oracle's own documentation says depot-to-depot shipments ignore country-specific expiry settings, a blind spot for cross-border transfers ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/life-sciences/clinical-one/sponsor-cro-information/manage-expiration-dates-lots.html)), and N-SIDE's lot-allocation and required-country rules work the same way ([N-SIDE](https://knowledge.n-side.com/supply-app/what-is-the-difference-between-label-group-lot-allocation-and-required-countries)). Agents arrived in 2025–26. Signant's Forecasting & Planning Assist (August 2026) flags expiry, lead-time and stock-out risk and changes configuration within existing user permissions ([Signant](https://signanthealth.com/forecasting-planning-assist)); Endpoint's elo uses knowledge graphs to trace root causes of shipment delays ([Endpoint](https://www.endpointclinical.com/insights/unlocking-the-value-of-ai-in-irt-practical-perspectives-from-vendors)); Suvoda's agentic RTSM builds studies under expert sign-off and claims up to 80% faster start-up ([Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/suvoda-announces-agentic-rtsm-enabling-the-reduction-of-clinical-trial-startup-timelines-by-up-to-0001); [Suvoda](https://www.suvoda.com/insights/all-news/agentic-ai-in-rtsm-redefines-how-clinical-trial-teams-spend-their-time)). Each agent stays inside its own vendor's data and none ingests external events or country rules; an industry expert expects AI to reach forecasting before day-to-day site supply ([Informa](https://informaconnect.com/irt-and-rtsm-systems-attract-investment-as-adoption-grows-among-cros-and-sponsors)), even as logistics vendors pitch governed autonomous reallocation inside guardrails ([XMPro](https://xmpro.com/solutions-library/ai-agent-library,decision-agents/pharmaceutical-cold-chain-supply-continuity-agent/)). The closest analogue to a shipment-level releasability check is Veeva Batch Release, which links change controls to country approval status and stops batches tied to a market with an open issue, for commercial products only ([Veeva](https://www.veeva.com/blog/from-excess-caution-to-true-jurisdictional-control-shifting-gears-on-market-shipping/)).

Import knowledge mostly lives in services. Marken markets a knowledgebase of approved import requirements ([Marken](https://www.marken.com/news/ensuring-smooth-importation-of-clinical-trial-materials-the-importance-of-accurate-customs-documentation)), Thermo Fisher acts as importer of record in more than 25 countries ([Patheon IOR FAQ](https://www.patheon.com/content/dam/temp_patheon/library/resources/ebook/eb-importer-of-record-frequently-asked-questions.pdf)) and names failure to secure shipment approval before import as a cause of delay ([PharmTech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/q-a-how-can-sponsors-respond-to-clinical-trial-customs-delays-)), and couriers publish operational updates during crises ([Marken](https://www.marken.com/news/supply-chain-operational-update-israel-jordan-lebanon)). Supply-chain risk monitors such as Prewave reportedly track more than 200 operational and regulatory risk types with no IMP or lot mapping, though that evidence is weak ([Tradeverifyd](https://tradeverifyd.com/resources/everstream-alternatives)), and a logistics vendor argues that most pharma control towers remain dashboards waiting for a person to act ([FourKites](https://www.fourkites.com/blogs/pharma-supply-chain-ai-execution/)). The competitive conclusion follows: **the event feed is the commodity layer, and the defensible layer is the mapping** from an event or rule to the authorised portfolio's shipments, facilities, routes and deadlines, plus the approval trail. SupplyShield should take official sources and, where a customer already licenses one, a commercial risk feed, rather than build a news operation of its own.

Veeva has the shortest path to closing the gap: RIM data, Batch Release logic, its own RTSM since buying Veracity Logic in 2021, clinical-operations agents scheduled for August 2026 and usage-based AI pricing ([Veeva RTSM](https://www.veeva.com/resources/veeva-acquires-veracity-logic-for-randomization-and-trial-supply-management-rtsm/); [Veeva AI agents](https://www.veeva.com/eu/resources/veeva-ai-agents-to-be-released-across-all-veeva-applications/)). IQVIA combines regulatory intelligence, its own IRT ([YES Press](https://yespress.io/cenduit)) and an agent platform launched in March 2026 ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/779604/iqvia-unveils-iqvia-ai-a-unified-agentic-ai-platform-powered-by-nvidia-to-improve-efficiency-and-decision-making-across-life-sciences)). RTSM vendors hold lot and depot data and lack only the regulatory and external layer, and couriers already hold the import know-how. The buyer is fragmented: clinical supply owns planning and the RTSM, regulatory affairs owns approvals, labels and intelligence subscriptions, Quality and the QP own release, and trade compliance or the importer of record owns permits. CTS Europe 2025 speakers included a head of clinical supply operations and a clinical supply chain director ([Pharmaceutical Technology](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/features/amid-deglobalising-trade-experts-seek-ai-safeguards-for-clinical-trial-supply/)), the natural champions. Prices are opaque: RTSM is bought per study with build and enrolment-linked fees ([Replior](https://abakus.replior.se/)), Signant bundles its agents ([Signant](https://signanthealth.com/forecasting-planning-assist)), and one vendor estimates enterprise RIM at $150k–400k a year ([DnXT](https://dnxtsolutions-b5fghth7fdakdadv.westus2-01.azurewebsites.net/2026/02/17/real-cost-enterprise-rim)). With RTSM vendors folding agents into per-study fees, a standalone tool must price against disruption the customer can see in its own records. No 2026 startup round was found in supply compliance; the money went to trial data, design and site operations ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/03/18/rivia-raises-15m-to-fix-the-broken-data-infrastructure-behind-clinical-trials/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/faro-ai-raises-37-3-million-series-b-as-platform-reaches-6-of-worlds-10-largest-pharma-companies/amp/)).

## SupplyShield should sell confirmed exposure, with people holding every switch

The evidence supports SupplyShield's positioning better than it supported RegChain's. The best-measured continuity shocks are external events that hit hubs, airspace and borders; the regulatory demand is continuity of treatment backed by documented risk review; and the open market space is mapping risk to specific shipments, facilities, routes and deadlines. SupplyShield's job is to answer one question per event (which of our authorised shipments, facilities, routes and deadlines does this touch, how sure are we, and who owns the response) and to treat a rule change and a hub closure as the same object: **an event with a geography, an effective window and an affected set**. The UK labelling cut-over and the Gulf hub closures then run through one engine, and RegChain's country-intelligence work survives as one event source among several rather than the headline. Scoping monitoring to the authorised active-trial portfolio also formalises a boundary the repository already draws, which warns against putting real country specifications into AI requests without company approval ([Screens l.97](https://github.com/Gpanaritis/trialReady/blob/6d04e99e416db14ed24a6882d206b8fc54b0cb4e/RegChain-AI-Screen-Guide.md?plain=1#L97)).

| SupplyShield rule | Evidence behind it | RegChain today | Change needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope comes only from the authorised active-trial portfolio | Regulatory-intelligence tools key on products, not shipments; vendor agents act within existing permissions | Fictional portfolio; portfolio prioritisation is real code | Read-only portfolio ingestion, with a record of who authorised each trial |
| Extra countries are recommended, never auto-enabled | Gulf hub closures exposed shipments far from the Gulf; Ukrainian supply moved through neighbours; the cross-border leg is weakest | No scope logic | Dependency graph of facilities, routes, hubs, borders and sea exposure; a stated reason per recommendation; a named person enables |
| Events are matched to shipments, facilities, routes and deadlines | No vendor maps events or rules to shipments; RTSM agents stay inside one system | Rule checks only, on fictional shipments | Matching on route, facility and date windows: dispatch, stock run-out, permit validity, shelf life |
| Observed, potential and confirmed stay separate | Industry counts sites "affected"; EMA serious-breach examples turn on confirmed facts | Blocked, confirm and ready, for rule checks | Three states with evidence rules for promotion; confirmation from carrier, depot or site |
| Agents propose mitigations with owners | Each recovery lever has its own rules; owners span supply, regulatory, Quality and trade compliance | Plans from a scripted planner; live mode never run | Live runs benchmarked against the scripted planner; one owner role per action |
| Humans approve changes to the country database, regulated systems and shipment instructions | Draft Annex 22; E6(R3) sponsor accountability; competitors' sign-off models | Approval workflow exists, but pending changes already drive checks | Checks run on approved versions only; pending changes shown as a what-if |

The rule to recommend extra countries, never switch them on, earns its place on both evidence and governance. The March 2026 closures exposed shipments to countries with no trial site anywhere near the Gulf, and Ukrainian supply moved through neighbouring states, so a monitor confined to trial countries is structurally blind. Yet each added country adds sources for regulatory SMEs to review and widens monitoring beyond what the company authorised. Recommending each country with its reason (a depot that supplies two trials, a hub on the route, a border with a conflict zone, current official advice) and having a named person enable it keeps the scope explainable and auditable. The triggers deserve different weights: hubs, airspace and facility dependencies have documented trial impact, while the record holds no trial-level impact from sea or port chokepoints, so **sea proximity should rank as a weaker reason** unless a facility or comparator flow depends on a port.

Mitigations should come from levers regulators already recognise, each tested against its own rules before a person sees it. The options are to reroute; swap to a batch QP-certified for the destination with enough shelf life left (E6(R3) 3.15.3(c)(v)); move the dispatch date within site storage limits; relabel at hospital pharmacies under CTR Article 61(5)(a) where the product stays within the same trial and country ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/536/oj)); ship direct to patients under E6(R3) 2.10.8 and the FDA's home-delivery answer, subject to national law; source locally where the product is marketed and the trial unblinded ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/172258/download); [CTCG](https://www.anm.ro/en/_/ANUNTURI%20IMPORTANTE/2022_04_CTCG_recommendation_to_sponsors_on_managing_the_impact_of_the_war_in_Ukraine_on_clinical_trials.pdf)); raise site buffers ahead of a looming disruption ([ContractPharma](https://www.contractpharma.com/?p=13465)); or escalate under E6(R3) 3.13.3 and CTR Articles 52 and 54. RegChain's specification already names the owner roles (regulatory SMEs approve; clinical supply, labelling, import/export and logistics implement; Quality verifies) ([Spec l.41](https://github.com/Gpanaritis/trialReady/blob/6d04e99e416db14ed24a6882d206b8fc54b0cb4e/RegChain-AI-Master-Specification.md?plain=1#L41)), and every proposal should carry one. The scripted planner becomes the benchmark: live agents justify their cost and risk only if they beat it on the same cases, which nobody has yet tested.

### Five dated disruptions make a ready-made backtest

Validation should replace the hackathon's fictional world with backtests against dated events: the Russian invasion (24 February 2022), Israel's airspace closure (13–22 June 2025), the Gulf hub closures (March 2026), the UK labelling cut-over (28 April 2026) and the Section 232 phase-in (31 July and 29 September 2026). Replaying them against a pilot customer's real shipment, facility and route history, read-only as the specification already plans ([Spec l.43](https://github.com/Gpanaritis/trialReady/blob/6d04e99e416db14ed24a6882d206b8fc54b0cb4e/RegChain-AI-Master-Specification.md?plain=1#L43)), measures what matters: how early SupplyShield would have raised each exposure, how many potential exposures became confirmed disruptions, and what it missed. Candidate measures follow the regulatory hooks: patients at risk of a missed dose in the next N days, days of usable supply at site against resupply lead time, time from observed event to exposure decision, and time to disposition after an excursion against Tufts' 57-hour mean. No standard continuity KPI set exists, and only stock-out risk, overage and waste have documented vendor use ([N-SIDE](https://www.n-side.com/supply-chain-clinical-trial-supply-overage-reduce-product-wastage-keeping-patients-service-level/); [Calyx](https://www.calyx.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/1.-Calyx-Brochure_Supply-Simulation_RGB_5.pdf)). Site resupply takes a couple of days while upstream stock-outs take weeks to months ([WSC 2019](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc19papers/011.pdf)), which sets the warning horizon an alert must beat. Willingness to pay should be tested separately, with a clinical supply leader as champion and regulatory affairs and Quality as approvers.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "did a rule change?" to "which of our shipments does this event touch, and who decides?". Sponsors answer it today with inventory: overage of 150–200% and roughly half of all kits wasted is what continuity costs when exposure cannot be seen shipment by shipment. That makes SupplyShield's most valuable long-term asset its ledger of confirmed disruptions rather than its agents. The industry publishes no per-shipment rate for holds, permit failures or excursions, so a tool that records how often potential exposures turn into confirmed disruptions would be the first to measure the problem it sells against, and the only honest basis for ever claiming that better visibility lets sponsors carry less buffer.

Regulation is turning human control from a constraint into a selling point: draft Annex 22, E6(R3)'s sponsor accountability and every competitor's sign-off model assume that the agent proposes and a person decides. The sharper risk is credibility. RegChain showed a world more finished than its evidence, with unlabelled simulations, a counter of hard-coded rows and a live run scripted before it happened. SupplyShield's first pilot should invert that pattern: label every simulated element, run the agents live before claiming they work, and let a customer's disruption history rather than a hackathon theme set the roadmap. Veeva and IQVIA hold most of the pieces, so the window for a vendor-neutral exposure layer is open now and will narrow as they assemble them.
